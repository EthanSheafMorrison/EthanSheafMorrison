## Overview

This project examines the phosphate trade between Western Sahara and Aotearoa New Zealand—a contentious supply chain that raises questions of settler colonialism, environmental justice, and Indigenous sovereignty. Developed with artist and designer [Matthew Galloway](http://matthew-galloway.co.nz), the project investigates how visual communication can expose extractive systems often hidden from public view.

I combined storytelling, spatial mapping, and advocacy design to build an interactive platform that traces the journey of phosphate from mine to field. By revealing the actors and infrastructures behind this trade, the project invites viewers to consider the complicity of agricultural systems in global injustices and opens space for critical reflection and solidarity with Saharawi self‑determination efforts.

## Approach

Bridging academic research and digital practice, the work maps datasets from NGOs like Western Sahara Resource Watch alongside historical and contemporary shipping records. It uses custom Mapbox interfaces to animate phosphate shipment routes, integrating interactive timelines and contextual overlays. Beyond data, the interface embeds audio, protest imagery, and citations to surface layered narratives, designed to be navigated non‑linearly.
